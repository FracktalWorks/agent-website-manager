Voice

Voice & compliance

How the practice sounds, and the lines it may not cross.

Write like this

LineWhy it works
Yes “Vitiligo, psoriasis, acne, hair loss and modern aesthetics”Names the conditions a patient searches for
Yes “45 years — 25 of them in the Army Medical Corps”Verifiable, specific, and it earns trust without adjectives
Yes “You don't need to know the name of the condition”Meets the patient where they are

Never write this

LineWhy it fails
No “Best dermatologist in Hyderabad”Superlative — prohibited by IMC advertising rules
No “Permanent cure for vitiligo”Cure claim, and clinically untrue
No “Thousands of happy patients”Unattributable number
No “Painless, guaranteed results”Guarantee — prohibited, and no procedure can promise it

Write the way a senior consultant speaks in a consulting room: short declarative sentences, no hedging, no hype. Lead with the patient’s own words — someone searches “white patches on skin”, not “vitiligo management protocol”.

Indian Medical Council advertising rules bind every page. No cure claims, no guarantees, no superlatives, no comparison to a named competitor. This is not a style preference — it is the condition of the practice advertising at all.
